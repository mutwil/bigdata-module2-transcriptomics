Could this overlap happen by chance?

Imagine choosing genes at random, rather than selecting them by differential expression. Would we get as many genes from our functional group? Repeat the experiment 100 times to find out.

Each draw selects n different genes: no replacement within a draw. We restore the entire background before the next draw. For RNA-seq, the background is not automatically every gene in the genome, and it includes the selected genes.

What should we conclude?

Repeat the 100 draws: the estimate changes because the samples are random, but the exact probability does not. Zero extreme draws does not prove that the probability is zero. A hundred draws give a rough demonstration, not a precise estimate of small p-values.

This tests one gene set. When testing many gene sets, adjust their p-values for multiple testing. Enrichment is an association; it does not by itself prove that a pathway is activated.

What is the statistical model?

Under the null model, all subsets of n genes from the N background genes are equally likely. With K gene-set members in that background, the overlap X follows a hypergeometric distribution. The enrichment p-value is P(X ≥ k). Its mean is n × K / N.

The simulator samples actual distinct gene IDs in every draw. Genes 1–K represent gene-set members. The exact probability is calculated from the hypergeometric distribution, independently of the 100 random draws. This is the same upper-tail test as a one-sided Fisher exact test on the corresponding 2 × 2 table.

Real gene selection can be affected by expression level, gene length and other biases; random sampling is the reference model, not a claim that differential expression is literally random.
