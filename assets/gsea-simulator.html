GSEA: walk the ranked gene list

Are the members of a functional group concentrated towards one end of a ranked gene list? Build a running score, then compare it with random gene sets. Unlike threshold-based enrichment, we keep the full ranked list—there is no significant-gene cutoff.

3. Would random gene sets give a score this extreme?

Keep the ranked list and K fixed. Randomly choose K different positions as gene-set members and recalculate the ES. Repeat to make a null distribution. This models preranked gene-set randomisation, not shuffling treatment labels.

Try these three patterns

Beginning: members occur first, producing a positive peak. End: non-members occur first, producing a negative trough. Middle: the curve first falls, then rises, then falls again. With a symmetric arrangement, the positive and negative excursions tie—there is no unique direction.

A score is not a p-value. Its significance depends on what the randomisations produce. Repeating 100 random sets changes the estimate; 1,000 usually makes it more stable. No extreme random scores does not mean the true probability is zero.

Scoring, ties and the scope of this simulator

Unweighted scoring: a member adds 1/K; a non-member subtracts 1/(N − K). Weighted scoring (exponent 1): a member adds |its ranking statistic| divided by the sum of |ranking statistics| over all members. Misses still subtract 1/(N − K). All weighted hits being zero makes the weighted score undefined; use unweighted scoring in that case.

For positive observed ES, the GSEA-style estimate is the fraction of non-negative null scores at least as large as the observed ES. For negative observed ES, it is the fraction of negative null scores at least as negative. This is a raw Monte Carlo fraction, not a plus-one-corrected estimate; small estimates based on few randomisations are unreliable.

Equal positive and negative excursions are explicitly shown as a tie (tolerance 10⁻¹⁰). We do not give a directional p-value for a tied observation. Choose the either-end comparison to test its maximum absolute deviation instead. For null-score sign bookkeeping, tied null curves use the negative excursion consistently. This convention is arbitrary and is not biological evidence of a direction.

The either-end comparison uses all randomisations and |ES|. It is an additional teaching test, not the standard GSEA nominal p-value. Random sets preserve K but do not preserve biological gene–gene correlations. Full GSEA can instead permute sample labels. This simulator does not calculate a normalised enrichment score (NES), FDR, or pathway activation.
